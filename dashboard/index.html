How the model works

Value, in words

For a protocol that records no token, implied value is its fitted fully diluted value (FDV) times its launch odds. Fitted FDV is TVL times a category multiple times 2.0. The category multiple is the geometric mean of two things: the snapshot's own shrunk median market-cap-to-TVL among live protocols of that category, and an external FDV-to-TVL comp from research — clamped between 0.03 and 1.0. The 2.0 is the median FDV-to-market-cap ratio across comparable launched tokens.

Launch odds are the chance a still-tokenless protocol launches within the next twelve months, from a decreasing-hazard survival fit on the peers' record. Odds fall with age — about 0.7 at thirty days, about 0.4 at one year, under 0.1 at three years for a $100M-TVL protocol with flat growth — rise slightly with TVL, and fall with fast seven-day growth.

Live-token rows are valued at their reported market cap where one is reported; where it is not, they are valued at the fitted FDV and flagged as such. They are labelled POSITION, not a farm.

Limitations, stated plainly

The value model is rough. Its ten-fold cross-validation median error is , barely better than the global-median baseline, and its older-to-newer held-out R² is — TVL alone explains little of a token's value. Implied values are model claims, not prices and not advice. A protocol is described as recording no token, never as having none: the snapshot only records what it lists. The snapshot is sealed as of the date above; nothing after it is reflected.
